The middle path, and the one that draws a rule you can actually apply: mono never appears in chrome — no eyebrows, no timecode, no meta lines, no counts. It appears only where a fixed character grid is doing real work, which is exactly two places.

This is the argument. A member pastes a chart from a songbook and it is already aligned by spaces — the app stores it as text and has no idea which chord belongs to which syllable. Render that in a proportional face and the alignment collapses. A1 and A2 both need the editor to become structured first.

05The rule, stated once
Mono is reserved for text whose horizontal position carries meaning, and for strings a human has to transcribe character by character. Everything else — every label, every number the app itself formatted — is Chivo. That rule is short enough to hold in your head while building components, which is the point of stating it rather than deciding case by case in milestone 2.
Wins
Gets you the warmth of A1 in every screen you actually look at, without breaking pasted chord charts or making admin tokens unreadable. Nothing has to change in the data model.
Costs
A third webfont for two narrow cases, and a rule a future contributor has to know — a design system with an exception in it is a design system people get wrong.
