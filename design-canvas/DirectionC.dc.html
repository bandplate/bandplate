editorial
Paper, not a screen. Sand ground with a halftone tooth, espresso ink, a single rust accent doing all the work. Reggae arrives through colour and print texture rather than through the lettering — which leaves the type free to be quiet enough to read lyrics on.
Why this one
Half of what this app shows is long text — lyrics, chords, notes — read at arm's length in a bright room. Dark ink on warm paper is the only ground that is genuinely comfortable for that, and it carries ROOST's existing sand palette forward instead of discarding it.
The tradeoff
It is the least literally reggae of the three, and a bright screen in a dim rehearsal room is unkind — so the dark counterpart has to be genuinely good here, not an afterthought. Gold drops to 2.1:1 on sand: graphic use only, never text.

Stems
Motion
Restrained: a 50ms stagger fading rows up, and titles crossfading through the view transition so a song title morphs from list to detail. No bounce, no glow.
Atmosphere
A 4px halftone dot grid gives the sand a tooth, a gold wash warms the top edge and olive settles into the bottom-left. Rules are hairlines; shadows barely exist.
Desktop
This is the direction that scales up best: song detail becomes a real two-column spread, lyrics and chords in one column at 19px, takes in the other.
