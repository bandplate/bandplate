The three messages, as they go out today

bandplate sends exactly three: the sign-in link (the only door in), the invite, and a one-line test at setup. Two of them are plain text with no HTML part at all; the third has one line of HTML.

1 · The sign-in link — HTML part
Your bandplate login link
from bandplate
Hi Vařič,

Use this link to sign in: http://localhost:4321/login/9xKQ2mWfVn3pLdA7RtYzB4cHsJ6uE1oN This link expires at 2026-09-09T12:49:33.412Z.

The whole message is one paragraph. No wordmark, no button, nothing that says who sent it beyond the subject line — and this is the ONLY way into the app.

The link text is the URL. A 60-character string carrying a single-use token, printed as the thing you are meant to click. It wraps badly and it looks like exactly the sort of link people are told not to click.

The expiry is an ISO timestamp. "2026-09-09T12:49:33.412Z" is machine output. Nobody reads it, and it is in UTC.

And it starts with a stray space — the expiry sentence is built with a leading space, so it runs on after the link with an odd gap.

2 · The sign-in link — plain-text part, and the stray space in situ
Your bandplate login link
from bandplate
Hi Vařič,

Use this link to sign in:
http://localhost:4321/login/9xKQ2mWfVn3pLdA7RtYzB4cHsJ6uE1oN
 This link expires at 2026-09-09T12:49:33.412Z.

The text part is the better of the two — it at least puts the link on its own line. The highlighted leading space is the same bug as above, visible here as an indent on the last line.

3 · The invite, and the setup test — NO html part at all
You've been added to bandplate
from bandplate
Hi Vařič,

You've been added to bandplate — your band's rehearsal and recording archive.

There's no password to set up. Go to http://localhost:4321/login, enter this address
(xaralis93@gmail.com), and we'll email you a link that signs you in.

http://localhost:4321/login
bandplate is set up
from bandplate
This is a test message confirming outbound mail works for your bandplate deployment.

The invite reads well — it explains that there is no password, which is the one thing a new member needs. It just arrives as bare monospace text while the sign-in mail arrives as HTML, so the two look like they came from different systems.

The setup test says nothing useful. It confirms mail works, which is its whole job, but it is the first thing a new deployer ever sees from their own install.

And the sign-in body exists TWICE in the codebase — once in the SMTP mailer, once in the HTTP one — as two copies of the same strings that nothing keeps in step.
