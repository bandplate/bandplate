The display face, again
Alfa Slab One is a single ultra-bold weight with tight apertures. At hero size that is the point; at row size — 18–19px, which is most of what you actually look at — the counters close up and Czech diacritics crowd the cap-height. Every option below is shown at the three sizes that matter, with the same Czech titles, so the comparison is the failing case rather than a specimen. Body copy stays Chivo and mono stays Spline Sans Mono throughout; only the display face changes.

Keep the face you picked, but stop using it at row size. Alfa Slab stays for the hero and the section title; take and song titles drop to Chivo 900, which is what the meta line beneath them is already set in.
Cheapest change by far, and it targets exactly where the collapse happens. But a list of takes is most of what you look at, so the slab stops being the thing you see.
What to look at
Read the Rows block in each column, not the hero. Check the ě and š in Píseň and Zkušebna, and whether the counters of a, e and o stay open. That is where Alfa Slab fails.
Where I'd land
T2 Zilla Slab — it keeps the crate-lettering character that made you pick Dubplate, and the weight axis means a row title at 600 has the air Alfa Slab never had at any size. T3 Vollkorn if legibility matters more than the label feeling; its stroke contrast handles diacritics best of the four. T4 is the honest cheap answer, but it quietly demotes the display face out of the screen you spend most time in.
